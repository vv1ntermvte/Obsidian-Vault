Study Title: Eco-Feedback in the Use of Generative AI

1. What was the study about?
	The study was supposed to investigate, how eco-feedback would influence the participants behavior when interacting with generative ai. For this, the study was split into two groups. For each group, the setup consisted of a ChatGPT instance and a tool for text-input in a separate window. The task of the participant was, to prompt ChatGPT to invent a short fairy tale. This fairy tale would then be submitted in the other tool an be evaluated. The participant would then receive a score for their submitted tale and get feedback on what to improve to increase their score. This could for example be to make the tale more exciting, add certain elements, like a dragon, to the story, or optimize the word count (if I remember correctly the desired length was 100 words). The participant could then use this feedback to optimize their prompt and revise the fairy tale an unlimited number of times. If the score satisfied the participant they could at all times decide to finish. 
	The Difference between the two groups was, that one group had an unmodified version of ChatGPT, while the other had an adapted version that included a little mascot in the corner of the ChatGPT interface, that made comments about the AIs resource consumption while the participant prompted and grew more and more desperate with the user typing more prompts.
	The Study aimed to examine whether the participants being informed about their energy consumption would be more mindful regarding their prompting behavior (i.e. stopping to prompt and being satisfied with their score earlier)
	What was not revealed to the participants beforehand was the fact, that the scoring system was entirely rigged. The score was not actually being calculated based on the submitted text but random within a certain range. The provided feedback was also made up. 
	After the participant finished, a short interview was conducted. I was part of the group with the mascot and was asked a number of questions, including whether I noticed the mascot itself, whether I read its comments, if that affected my prompting behavior, if I felt an incentive to achieve a high score and if that was incentive was influenced by me reading the comments.  
	
2. What were the dependent and independent variables?
	 The independent variable was the provided version of ChatGPT. Due to this, one group received feedback on the environmental costs of their prompts, while the other didn't.
	 The dependent variables, i.e. the variables that were observed and measured, can only be guessed, but likely were, for one, the number of prompts a participant issued (i.e. the difference in issued prompts between the two groups was probably measured) and the results from the conducted interviews.
3. Which limitations of the study do you see with regard to the objective of the study?
	One Problem I noticed was, that the goal of the study was, at least to me but I would imagine that this holds for other participants as well, rather clear during the study itself. Of course it is hard to disguise what the study actually wants to examine, but a participant knowing what is expected of them could already lead to them acting in certain ways. Another problem I see is actually, that the score was only mocked an didn't actually increase with the participant refining their prompts. While I didn't know the exact cause of this, after only a few prompts it was already clear that further refining my fairy tale was not worth the effort, because it didn't seem to have a reliable effect on the score. This eliminated any agency towards getting a high score that might have been there beforehand, because I knew it was useless to try anyways. If the study involves measuring the amount of prompts a participant issues before finishing, this would be a problem because it cannot be clearly identified, if a small number of prompts was caused because the participant was conscious about the environmental effects of their prompts or due to them noticing that prompting won't increase their score and therefore exiting early. I feel like actually implementing a strategy to evaluate the fairy tale would lead to more concrete results, because small amounts of prompts would now with a higher certainty be caused due to the participant valuing the usage of less prompts higher than achieving a high score and not due to them giving up.
