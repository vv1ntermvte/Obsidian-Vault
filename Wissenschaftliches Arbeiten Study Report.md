Study Title: Eco-Feedback in the Use of Generative AI

1. What was the study about?
	The study was supposed to investigate, how eco-feedback would influence the participants behavior when interacting with generative ai. For this, the study was split into two groups. For each group, the setup consisted of a ChatGPT instance and a tool for text-input in a separate window. The task of the participant was, to prompt ChatGPT to invent a short fairy tale. This fairy tale would then be submitted in the other tool an be evaluated. The participant would then receive a score for their submitted tale and get feedback on what to improve to increase their score. This could for example be to make the tale more exciting, add certain elements, like a dragon, to the story, or optimize the word count (if I remember correctly the desired length was 100 words). The participant could then use this feedback to optimize their prompt and revise the fairy tale an unlimited number of times. If the score satisfied the participant they could at all times decide to finish. 
	The Difference between the two groups was, that one group had an unmodified version of ChatGPT, while the other had an adapted version that included a little mascot in the corner of the ChatGPT interface, that made comments about the AIs resource consumption while the participant prompted and grew more and more desperate with the user typing more prompts.
	The Study aimed to examine whether the participants being informed about their energy consumption would be more mindful regarding their prompting behavior (i.e. stopping to prompt and being satisfied with their score earlier)
	What was not revealed to the participants beforehand was the fact, that the scoring system was entirely rigged. The score was not actually being calculated based on the submitted text but random within a certain range. The provided feedback was also made up. 
	
2. What were the dependent and independent variables?
	 The independent variable was the provided version of ChatGPT.
