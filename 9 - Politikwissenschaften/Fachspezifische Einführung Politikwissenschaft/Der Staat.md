# Lecture Notes

### Government ≠ State

The government is the temporary institution in charge of making government decisions. The state can prevail once the government changes and isn't dependent on it. Often times, structures of the states like various institutions don't change fundamentally for very long times. This distinction between government and state is important, because while the legitimacy of the state is accepted, while that of a given government is not necessarily. 
### Rise of the modern State

Compared to the emergence of human civilization, the concept of states is a relatively new invention, and even with the emergence of the first states, for a long time, there existed many different kinds of state concepts. Today, however, the whole world is organized via nation states.
##### Max Weber's definition of the modern state

Weber defines the modern state by its monopoly over the legitimate use of physical force within given boundaries. 

How could states come into a position having a monopoly over the legitimate use of violence? According to sociologist Charles Tilly, in case of the European continent, the reason for this lies within the political competition between empires emerging from the collapse of religious authority between the 15th and 17th century. States where created not by design, but just so happened to emerge, because they where good at coercion and extraction, which where needed to finance the military ambitions of the empires. Tilly argues, that modern states of that time essentially function like a legal mafia, as they artificially or willfully create threats to then sell protection from them in the form of taxes. In consequence, only those states that where successful at this could prevail.
